The scene engine

Follow the conversation, not just the quotation marks.

BookCast resolves each quote with scene context: paragraph boundaries, action beats, pronouns, vocatives, presence, and addressee cues. Explicit “said” tags always win.

Paragraph-scoped tags

A name after a blank line belongs to the next beat. Tags only count in the quote’s own paragraph, so a distant mention can’t steal the line.

Pronoun turn-taking

A bare “she said” resolves to the last speaker of that gender — never the other one. First-person narrators stay out of third-person pronoun matches.

Presence & addressees

Distant mentions and objects of prepositions don’t become speakers. “Your mom…” points at the person being addressed, not at Mom.

Family & group scenes

Four-person dinners, rapid back-and-forth, and parent-child turns stay distinct. The engine tracks who is present, who just spoke, and who is being answered.

Language-aware fallback

French, Spanish, German, and Italian class scenes are detected as scenes of their own, instead of leaking named relatives into a conversation they aren’t in.

Minor voices, not mystery voices

Unnamed teachers, students, and strangers get gender-matched minor voices until the book names them. No single generic “Unknown” voice for the whole cast.

Deterministic by default. The optional LLM pass only revisits uncertain fallback lines. It never overrides an explicit tag — and the default pipeline stays fast and offline.

From shelf to headphones

Four steps. No cloud account.

Bring a book you already own. BookCast handles the analysis, the cast, the voices, and the long render — with resume built in for the parts that take a while.

Questions

Good to know before you cast.

Do I need an LLM or a paid API key?

No. The deterministic scene engine is the default and the measured offline path. The optional LLM mode is opt-in, works with free/local models such as Ollama on a Colab GPU, and only revisits uncertain fallback lines. Explicit “said” tags are never overridden.

What books does BookCast work with?

Text-based EPUBs and PDFs. EPUB is the better input because chapter structure and paragraphs are already clean. PDFs work best when they contain selectable text; scanned image-only PDFs need OCR first. Use only books you have the right to convert.

Does my book leave my machine?

The app, cast, progress, and audio stay on your machine. If you choose Edge TTS, the text being spoken is sent to Microsoft’s speech service. Choose Kokoro for a fully offline render. No BookCast account or upload step is involved.

How accurate is the speaker attribution?

It is probabilistic, not magic. The scene engine is the default because it measured best among the offline engines on public-domain novels, and it is tuned against a regression suite. When it gets a line wrong, the cast editor lets you reassign that quote or fix the character without re-rendering the whole book.

Can I stop a long render and continue later?

Yes. Renders are chunked and resumable. Cancelling a job keeps finished chunks, and a restart skips them. Edits that affect a chapter delete only that chapter’s cached chunks, so the next render redoes just what changed.

Can I make one MP3 per chapter instead of one big file?

Yes. Use the per-chapter output option in the studio or --per-chapter on the CLI. Chapter ranges also work, so you can render 1–3, 6–8 first and come back for the rest.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no BookCast account, subscription, or per-minute billing. Edge TTS is free to use, and Kokoro is an open offline voice engine. You provide the hardware and the books.
